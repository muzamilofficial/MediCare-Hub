During heavy monsoon downpours and urban flooding in Karachi and across Sindh, sewage lines frequently mix with underground domestic water pipes. Within days, clinics see a surge in **acute gastroenteritis (diarrhea and vomiting), XDR Typhoid, Hepatitis A & E (jaundice), eye infections, and fungal skin rashes**.

### 1. Make Every Drop of Drinking Water Safe

Even if tap or filter water looks clear after rain, microscopic bacteria and viruses may be present:

- **Rolling Boil for 3 Minutes:** Boil all drinking water—even filtered water—until large bubbles roll continuously for 1 to 3 minutes, then let it cool in a covered steel or glass container.
- **Use Boiled Water for Brushing & Baby Formula:** Always use boiled water when preparing infant formula milk, washing fruits/salads, and brushing children's teeth.
- **Clean Underground & Rooftop Tanks:** After rainy spells end, have your underground water tank drained, scrubbed, and chlorinated.

### 2. Protect Children from Gastroenteritis & Dehydration

If a child or elderly family member develops loose motions or vomiting:

- Start **WHO low-osmolarity ORS** immediately—give a few spoonfuls after every loose stool.
- Continue breastfeeding for infants, and offer soft, easily digestible foods like **banana, plain khichdi, yogurt (*dahi*), and boiled rice water**.
- **Avoid street food, cut roadside fruits, raw chutneys, and ice (*baraf*)** from unknown sources during rainy months.

### 3. Foot & Skin Care After Walking Through Rainwater

- Floodwater carries bacteria and sharp debris. If you must walk through standing water, wash your feet and legs thoroughly with **soap and clean water** as soon as you reach home, and dry carefully between the toes.
- **Diabetic patients** must never walk barefoot or wade through rainwater, as even a tiny cut can turn into a serious diabetic foot infection.
- If a cut occurs, wash it immediately and check whether a **Tetanus Toxoid (TT) booster injection** is needed.
