Every year between July and November, following heavy monsoon rains across Karachi, Hyderabad, and Punjab, cases of **Dengue fever** rise sharply. Because the *Aedes aegypti* mosquito breeds in clean, stagnant water inside homes and rooftops, simple household precautions can prevent serious illness in your family.

### How Dengue Spreads Around Your Home

Unlike ordinary night mosquitoes, the dengue mosquito bites primarily during **early morning hours (sunrise to 10:00 AM)** and **late afternoon before maghrib (4:00 PM to 7:00 PM)**. Common breeding spots in Pakistani homes include:

- Uncovered overhead water tanks and underground sumps
- Water trays underneath indoor plants, room coolers, and AC outdoor units
- Old tires, buckets, or plastic containers left on the roof after rain

### Early Symptoms to Watch For

Dengue typically begins suddenly 4 to 7 days after a mosquito bite. Watch closely for:

1. **High-grade fever (102°F to 104°F)** accompanied by chills
2. **Severe headache and pain behind the eyes** (retro-orbital pain)
3. **Intense muscle, bone, and joint aches** ("breakbone fever")
4. **Nausea, loss of appetite, or mild skin rash**

### Critical Home Care Rules: What to Do & What to Avoid

If someone in your household develops a high fever during dengue season, follow these golden medical guidelines:

- **Use ONLY Paracetamol (Panadol/Calpol)** for fever and body pain, strictly within the dose advised by your doctor.
- **NEVER take Aspirin (Disprin), Ibuprofen (Brufen), Diclofenac, or Mefenamic Acid (Ponstan).** These painkillers thin the blood, irritate the stomach lining, and can trigger dangerous internal bleeding when platelets are low.
- **Prioritize Hydration:** Give plenty of oral rehydration salts (ORS), fresh coconut water, apple juice, homemade soup, and clean boiled water.
- **Order a Timely Lab Check:** A **CBC (Complete Blood Count)** and **Dengue NS1 Antigen test** on Day 2 or Day 3 of fever helps your physician monitor hematocrit (blood concentration) and platelet trends accurately.

### Red Flag Emergency Signs

Seek immediate medical care if you notice persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain, bleeding from the gums or nose, black stools, extreme drowsiness, or sudden dizziness when standing up—especially when the fever starts coming down around Day 4 or Day 5.
