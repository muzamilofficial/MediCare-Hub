Timely vaccination is the single most effective shield parents can give their newborn and growing child against life-threatening diseases in Pakistan—including **Polio, Tuberculosis, Pneumonia, Rotavirus diarrhea, Measles, and Drug-Resistant Typhoid**.

### Key Milestones in Pakistan's Immunization Schedule

1. **At Birth (Within 24 Hours):**
   - **BCG:** Protects against severe childhood Tuberculosis.
   - **OPV-0:** Oral Polio Vaccine first dose.
   - **Hepatitis B (Birth Dose):** Prevents mother-to-baby liver infection.

2. **At 6, 10, and 14 Weeks:**
   - **Pentavalent Vaccine (3 doses):** Covers Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis (Whooping Cough), Hepatitis B, and Hib meningitis.
   - **Pneumococcal (PCV) Vaccine:** Protects lungs against severe bacterial pneumonia.
   - **Rotavirus Drops (6 & 10 weeks):** Prevents severe watery diarrhea and hospitalization in infants.
   - **IPV (Injectable Polio):** Given at 14 weeks alongside oral drops for double immunity.

3. **At 9 Months:**
   - **Measles-Rubella (MR-1):** First dose against Measles (*Khasra*).
   - **Typhoid Conjugate Vaccine (TCV):** Crucial in Sindh and Punjab where extensively drug-resistant (XDR) typhoid is common.

4. **At 15 to 18 Months:**
   - **Measles-Rubella (MR-2)** second dose, plus **Chickenpox (Varicella)**, **Hepatitis A**, and **DTP/Pentavalent Booster**.

### Caring for Your Baby After Vaccination

- A mild fever (up to 100.5°F) or slight redness at the thigh injection site for 24 to 48 hours is normal and shows the immune system is building protection.
- Give weight-appropriate **Paracetamol drops** as advised by your pediatrician, and apply a clean, cool damp cloth to the injection spot.
- **Breastfeed frequently** to keep the infant comforted and hydrated.
- Always store your child's **Vaccination Card** in a plastic sleeve and bring it to every clinic visit—it is also required for school admissions and travel visas.
